Connect Agora to the Slack workspace your team already talks in. Three things follow: a channel hears the events that need a human, your inbox arrives as a direct message, and an Agora link pasted into a conversation unfurls into a small preview of the issue.

The design rule behind all of it is **quiet by default**. A new channel hears failures, QA verdicts and review verdicts — nothing else — because the way every chat integration dies is volume.

## What the integration does

| Surface | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Channel notifications** | An owner or admin picks channels and what each one hears. A fresh channel starts on `failed`, `qa_verdict` and `review_verdict`. |
| **Personal DMs** | Inbox items (assignment, mention, comment, agent done) arrive as a Slack DM, for anyone who has connected their own Slack account. |
| **Link unfurls** | Pasting an Agora issue or project link shows its identifier, title, status, assignee and priority, with a button back into Agora. |
| **Release webhook** | Unchanged and separate. The Slack **Incoming Webhook** connector in **Settings → Integrations → Release** keeps `release:shipped` and `deploy:recorded`; the Slack app never claims those events, so there is nothing to double-post. |

## Connect the workspace (owner / admin)

1. Open **Settings → Integrations → Slack**.
2. Click **Connect Slack**. Slack's consent screen opens in a new tab.
3. Approve, and Slack sends you back to the same settings page with the workspace connected.

The consent screen asks for `chat:write`, `chat:write.public`, `links:read`, `links:write`, `channels:read`, `groups:read`, `im:write` and `team:read`. There is **no message-history scope of any kind** — the app cannot read your conversations.

<Callout type="info">
If the card says **Slack is not available**, the deployment has no Slack app configured. A self-hosted Agora brings its own: `AGORA_SLACK_CLIENT_ID`, `AGORA_SLACK_CLIENT_SECRET`, `AGORA_SLACK_SIGNING_SECRET` and `AGORA_SLACK_SECRET_KEY` must all be set. See [Environment variables](/environment-variables).
</Callout>

## Route events to a channel

Under the connection, pick a channel and click **Add channel**. It starts on the quiet default; tick anything else you want.

| Event | What it means | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `failed` | An agent task failed | On |
| `qa_verdict` | A QA gate failed | On |
| `review_verdict` | A review asked for changes | On |
| `assigned` | An issue was assigned to someone | Off |
| `mentioned` | Someone was @-mentioned | Off |
| `agent_done` | An agent finished a task | Off |
| `status_changed` | An issue changed status | Off |
| `created` | An issue was created | Off |

Passing QA and passing reviews are not sent to channels. Teams that want the good news turn on `agent_done`, which is exactly that signal.

Comments are **never** routed to a channel. A comment is context for one person; in a room it is the thing that makes people mute.

<Callout type="warning">
Invite the Agora app to a private channel before routing it. A public channel works without an invite; a private one does not, and the post fails silently in Slack.
</Callout>

## Connect your own account

Open **Settings → Notifications → Slack** and click **Connect Slack**. That consent screen asks for your identity only — no bot permissions.

Connecting does two things:

- **Inbox DMs.** New inbox items arrive as a Slack DM. They follow the notification preferences on that same page: a muted group never creates an inbox item, so it never becomes a DM either. There is no second set of switches to keep in sync.
- **Unfurls.** Link previews are authorised as the person who posted the link, so connecting is what lets you see them.

## Link unfurls

Paste an issue link — `https://your-agora/acme/issues/MUL-123` — into a channel and it expands into the identifier, title, status, assignee, priority and project, plus an **Open in Agora** button. Project links unfurl to the project name.

What does *not* appear: the description. An unfurl renders to everyone in the channel, including people who are not Agora users, so the body of an issue stays in Agora.

Who sees what:

- **Not connected, or not a member of that workspace** — Slack shows *you* a private "connect your account" prompt. Nothing is posted to the channel.
- **Connected, but the issue is not yours** — the same private prompt. The [issue-visibility gate](/members-roles) applies unchanged: if you could not open the issue in Agora, its title does not reach the channel through you.
- **A link to a workspace this Slack team is not connected to** — nothing happens at all.

## Disconnect

**Settings → Integrations → Slack → Disconnect** stops Agora posting and removes the channel routes. The installation row is kept for audit and reconnecting restores it.

Removing the app inside Slack has the same effect from the other direction: Agora marks the installation revoked the moment Slack says the token is gone.

<Callout type="info">
One Slack workspace can back several Agora workspaces (an agency, or one workspace per product). Disconnecting one of them never uninstalls the app for its siblings.
</Callout>
